## Design Context

### Users

Developers who use Claude Code, Codex, or both—often across several accounts—and need to understand remaining usage or switch accounts without leaving their terminal workflow. The interface lives in the macOS menu bar, so every interaction should be glanceable, quick, and comfortable in a narrow popover.

### Brand Personality

Calm, compact, and characterful. Vibecom Bar should feel trustworthy enough to handle account credentials, precise enough for daily developer use, and warm enough that the Claude and Codex companions give it a recognizable personality.

### Aesthetic Direction

A highly refined, native Apple utility with the density and restraint of Control Center: layered system materials, crisp SF typography, gentle depth, strong alignment, and meaningful color used sparingly. Support light and dark appearances. Provider identity should come from small custom companion marks rather than oversized branding. Avoid generic dashboard cards, neon developer-tool styling, decorative gradients, and excessive glass effects.

### Design Principles

1. Make the most important fact—the account's remaining usage—readable in one glance.
2. Prefer compact rows, strong alignment, and progressive disclosure over stacked cards and repeated labels.
3. Give Claude and Codex distinct, friendly companion marks without impersonating official provider artwork.
4. Use one restrained motion language for navigation, refresh feedback, privacy changes, and progress updates; honor Reduce Motion.
5. Treat privacy as a first-class mode: account identities can be obscured everywhere from one setting.
6. Preserve native macOS behavior, accessibility, light/dark appearance, and keyboard usability.
